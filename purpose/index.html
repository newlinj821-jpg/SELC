Our purpose / What we care about

WE ARE HERE TO BUILD.

Sanctum Eros is more than a play party or a room full of equipment. We are an independent, queer-owned, 18+ kink organization creating places for people to learn, build trust, meet each other, and find community. Our private BDSM dungeon and dungeon events are 21+.

01 / Why SELC exists

MORE THAN A VENUE.

Good kink organizations need people who do the work between events. Someone has to welcome new faces, answer uncomfortable questions, build classes worth attending, notice when a system has stopped serving people, and help repair what has gone wrong. Those responsibilities do not disappear when a party ends.

The Sanctum Eros Leadership Committee exists to give that work structure, continuity, and more than one perspective. It is part of Sanctum Eros—not a separate club and not a substitute for the Founder. Five Representative seats, including two online-first positions, give members real ways to contribute. Up to two of those Representatives can serve as Coordinators. Eros remains Founder and Chair, with final responsibility for the organization and its venue.

We want this to feel like a community built by people rather than a calendar managed behind closed doors. That means listening, sharing useful information, developing ideas, and being willing to explain the decisions that affect the people who trust us.

02 / What we care about

PRINCIPLES THAT SHOW UP.

Consent

Clear, informed, ongoing.

Consent matters during conversation, negotiation, demonstrations, play, and everyday community interaction. It can be withdrawn. No title, reputation, authority, or relationship overrides another person's boundaries.

Education

Skill without gatekeeping.

Practical teaching should explain not only what to do, but what can go wrong, what to watch for, and when to stop. Beginners deserve good information; experienced kinksters should remain curious and accountable.

Community

Belonging beyond a scene.

Conversation, friendship, volunteering, accessible resources, and ordinary social connection matter. Being here should not require having a play partner, performing a role, or participating sexually.

Privacy

Trust is not publicity.

We treat members' identities, experiences, attendance, and sensitive concerns as information entrusted to us. Private venue details and incident records are restricted; not every leader needs to see everything.

Accountability

Take concerns seriously.

A concern deserves a way to be heard without rumor or retaliation. Staff reports, IA investigations, and Ban Reports are different processes with documented authority, review, and opportunities to address conflicts of interest.

Accessibility

Make room for real people.

We work to reduce barriers around disability, experience, finances, scheduling, and online participation. Access needs are part of event and education planning, not an inconvenience added afterward.

03 / How we lead

AUTHORITY WITH RESPONSIBILITY.

SELC Representatives contribute to education, committee proposals, event operations, outreach, and the practical details of maintaining a community. Coordinators keep that work moving and have additional authority. Staff and Dungeon Monitors still have meaningful operational authority, but they are not automatically SELC members. Specialist Internal Affairs duties require separate approval and certification.

Leadership is not a shortcut around consent or privacy. We distinguish an incident report from a Ban Report; we document decisions; and we require independent review when a decision-maker has a conflict. We do not equate an allegation with a finding. When a pending Ban Report places admission on hold, the restriction should have a defined review path rather than disappearing into an inbox.

Sanctum Eros also wants to learn from other organizations. Community Leaders can apply to collaborate on education, practical organizing, and, under carefully controlled conditions, safety information. No external organization receives our membership database or private case files automatically.

Our aim is neither perfection nor the appearance of perfection. It is to create processes people can understand, question, improve, and rely on.

04 / Who can participate

YOU DO NOT NEED TO KNOW EVERYTHING.

You can contribute by teaching, volunteering, reviewing ideas, offering feedback, or helping others feel welcome. Some work happens at events; some happens online. Curiosity and follow-through count.
