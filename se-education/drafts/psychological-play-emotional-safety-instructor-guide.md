# Psychological Play: Negotiation & Emotional Safety

**SE Education · Instructor guide v1 · DRAFT FOR SELC REVIEW · NOT PUBLISHED**

Duration: 120 minutes · Intermediate · Suggested 16 attendees · Discussion and verbal scenario exercises only. Instructor: educator experienced in psychological play negotiation and trauma-aware facilitation; clinical subjects referred to qualified professionals.

## Learning outcomes

Students will: distinguish consensual psychological play from coercion; negotiate exact language and topics; explain consent revocation and distress indicators; plan an end-of-roleplay transition; draft an aftercare and follow-up plan; and identify escalation resources.

## Prepare the classroom

Provide accessible seating, large-print worksheets, a quiet area, anonymous question cards, green/amber/red/unknown topic matrices and fictional scenario cards. No required personal disclosure, pairing, touching or live scenes. Verify staff escalation channels and confidentiality expectations.

## Detailed instructor run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10: Welcome and boundaries

**Say:** “Psychological play can involve intense themes; that is why we teach explicit agreements, not assumptions.”

**Do:** Explain no touching, no personal trauma disclosures, anonymous questions, quiet space and leave-anytime option.

**Check learning:** Ask for a hypothetical noncontact opt-out response.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 00:10–00:25: What psychological play includes

**Say:** “A theme may be attractive in fiction or roleplay and unacceptable outside the agreed scene.”

**Do:** Compare fictional teasing, status roleplay, anticipation and taboo language without acting them out.

**Check learning:** Students separate fictional role from real-world permission.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 00:25–00:45: Language and topic matrix

**Say:** “A broad preference does not authorize every word, insult or topic. Specific boundaries matter.”

**Do:** Teach green / amber / red / unknown matrix for language, settings, observers, duration and excluded themes. Unknown means ask first.

**Check learning:** Create a fictional matrix and mark three excluded topics.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 00:45–01:00: Negotiating a scene

**Say:** “Agree on exactly what happens, who is present, duration, interruptions, stop signals, privacy and how the scenario ends.”

**Do:** Read a noncontact two-person negotiation where a fictional authority scene lasts five minutes and participants agree on check-ins and an end signal.

**Check learning:** Find the missing details in 'I like humiliation'.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 01:00–01:10: Break

**Say:** “You may take a break or ask a question anonymously.”

**Do:** Offer quiet seating, water and written questions.

**Check learning:** No forced sharing.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 01:10–01:25: Recognizing overwhelm

**Say:** “Silence, confusion, dissociation-like signs or unexpected distress require stopping and checking safety; do not diagnose someone's response.”

**Do:** Discuss hypothetical scenarios of freezing, a word becoming hurtful, social pressure and a broken stop agreement.

**Check learning:** Name the first appropriate intervention.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 01:25–01:40: Ending and repair

**Say:** “The fictional frame should end deliberately. Ask about quiet, conversation, reassurance or distance; never presume touch.”

**Do:** Roleplay a spoken de-role conversation, an agreed check-in and respectful next-day follow-up.

**Check learning:** Draft one sentence inviting feedback without demanding reassurance.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 01:40–01:52: Case-based practice

**Say:** “Consent remains revocable even in a rehearsed fantasy. Any renegotiation happens outside pressure and roleplay.”

**Do:** Learners write a short negotiation for a purely hypothetical scene, including excluded words, stop signal, aftercare and privacy.

**Check learning:** Peer feedback is optional; individual worksheets remain private.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

### 01:52–02:00: Assessment and closing

**Say:** “Successful psychological play depends on consent, responsive stopping and appropriate support, not how far people can push a boundary.”

**Do:** Complete six-question knowledge check, discuss referral options and offer anonymous feedback.

**Check learning:** Exit ticket: one consent condition, one stop condition, and one follow-up action.

**Contingency:** If a student declines, offer silent observation or a self-guided worksheet. If someone appears distressed, stop the exercise, offer privacy and appropriate assistance without asking for a public explanation.

## Student follow-along worksheet

1. Identify a fictional psychological-play scenario and write its exact negotiated boundaries. Avoid personal details.
2. List topics or words that are acceptable, conditional, excluded or not yet discussed.
3. Identify a clear pause phrase, stop phrase and what happens immediately after either one.
4. Describe how the fictional participants would exit their roles, choose aftercare and agree on subsequent communication.
5. Explain how a facilitator should respond if a participant stops engaging or asks to end the scene.

## Six-question knowledge check (private instructor key)

1. **Does a general interest in degradation authorize all insulting language?**
   - A. Yes
   - B. No, exact language and contexts require agreement **[correct]**
   - C. Only among established partners
   - D. Only at private events
   - **Explanation:** Specific negotiation is necessary.

2. **What if a person freezes and is not responding?**
   - A. Continue roleplay
   - B. Stop, assess safety and allow support **[correct]**
   - C. Ask them to perform harder
   - D. Assume consent continues
   - **Explanation:** Loss of responsiveness is a stop condition.

3. **What does an unknown topic mean on the boundary matrix?**
   - A. Automatically allowed
   - B. Not discussed; seek explicit permission first **[correct]**
   - C. A soft yes
   - D. Always a hard no
   - **Explanation:** Unknown does not grant permission.

4. **Should an attendee have to reveal trauma to opt out?**
   - A. Yes
   - B. No, declining needs no explanation **[correct]**
   - C. Only in an advanced class
   - D. Only to a Dominant
   - **Explanation:** Autonomy and privacy remain essential.

5. **Which aftercare approach fits this subject?**
   - A. A mandatory embrace
   - B. Individualized agreement with an option for space **[correct]**
   - C. No follow-up
   - D. Only the top selects
   - **Explanation:** Individual needs vary.

6. **Can a roleplay identity override a genuine refusal?**
   - A. Yes
   - B. No **[correct]**
   - C. Only if agreed months ago
   - D. Only in a 24/7 structure
   - **Explanation:** Consent is ongoing and revocable.

## Common instructor questions

**“What if a participant previously agreed to intense roleplay?”** The actual person still retains the right to stop or change their mind. **“Can I use a real event as a scenario?”** Avoid identifying individuals without their permission. **“Does the class teach trauma processing?”** No. This is kink education, not treatment. **“Can I practice on a classmate?”** Not in this session; it is a discussion-based classroom.

## Evaluation

A suggested pass is five correct of six with missed safety or consent questions reviewed. Completion demonstrates basic comprehension, not professional or clinical qualification.
