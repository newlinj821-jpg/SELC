# Teaching Kink: Educator Facilitation Lab

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `educator-facilitation-lab`
- Level: Intermediate
- Duration: 120 minutes; suggested maximum 14 learners
- Delivery: Teaching practice, lesson planning and observed micro-lessons
- Educator qualification: Experienced adult educator and SELC education coordinator
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. Write measurable learning outcomes and an accessible outline.
2. Design optional participation with no forced disclosure or touch.
3. Explain the difference between instructor competence and technical proficiency.
4. Deliver a five-minute micro-lesson with a clear safety boundary.
5. Develop assessments, source citations, accessibility measures and feedback questions.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. Hold all participant data confidentially and avoid identifiable anecdotes. Prepare an anonymized question route and ensure demonstrations remain within the announced scope.

**Bring:**
- Blank lesson templates
- Timer and accessible presentation equipment
- Peer-review rubrics
- Anonymous evaluation forms
- Educational source and attribution checklist

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Set the culture (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Teaching skills can be practiced. Being expert at doing a thing is not the same as being prepared to teach it.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Give consent and participation expectations.

**Prompt / check:** Ask for a one-line learning outcome.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Objectives and scope (15 minutes)

**Say:** “A good objective says what a learner can do by the end, not just what the instructor will talk about.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Transform broad topics into observable objectives.

**Prompt / check:** Each participant writes three.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:40 · Building a run-of-show (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Plan opening, context, demonstration, learner activity, assessment, buffer and closing. Estimate actual timing.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Show an original instructor template.

**Prompt / check:** Participants map 90 minutes on a timeline.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:40–00:55 · Consent and accessible teaching (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Teaching is not consent to be touched or used as a demonstration volunteer. Establish opt-out routes and content notices.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Practice an invitation to participate that accepts a no gracefully.

**Prompt / check:** Students write one accessible alternative.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:55–01:05 · Break (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Prepare micro-lessons.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Check needs privately.

**Prompt / check:** No forced personal stories.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:05–01:25 · Micro-lesson delivery (20 minutes)

**Say:** “Be clear, check understanding, explain limits of expertise, and recognize when to stop.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Small groups deliver five-minute segments on harmless neutral subjects; peers use a rubric.

**Prompt / check:** Observe pace, accessibility and clear stop points.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:25–01:43 · Assessments and evidence (18 minutes)

**Say:** “Assess reasoning and safety decisions rather than memorization alone. Distinguish a completion statement from skill certification.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Rewrite poor multiple-choice questions and explain answers.

**Prompt / check:** Students create two scenario questions.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:43–01:55 · Feedback and revision (12 minutes)

**Say:** “Treat feedback as information. Record changes, source dates and an approval path.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Facilitator models neutral critique and a second-draft improvement.

**Prompt / check:** Peer-reviewed mini outline.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:55–02:00 · Close (5 minutes)

**Say:** “Approved educators still need course-specific approval and supervision where applicable.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Quiz and SELC workflow description.

**Prompt / check:** Submit lesson concept and development needs.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

## Learner handout / follow-along worksheet

**Before class:** Write one thing you hope to understand. You are not required to share your answer.

**During class:** Record the vocabulary and decision points that matter to you, then complete the following:

1. **Outcome 1:** Write measurable learning outcomes and an accessible outline. Write a hypothetical example and one question you would ask before acting.

2. **Outcome 2:** Design optional participation with no forced disclosure or touch. Write a hypothetical example and one question you would ask before acting.

3. **Outcome 3:** Explain the difference between instructor competence and technical proficiency. Write a hypothetical example and one question you would ask before acting.

4. **Outcome 4:** Deliver a five-minute micro-lesson with a clear safety boundary. Write a hypothetical example and one question you would ask before acting.

5. **Outcome 5:** Develop assessments, source citations, accessibility measures and feedback questions. Write a hypothetical example and one question you would ask before acting.

**Personal planning:** What information would you need, what boundary would you set, which stop signal would you use, what accessibility adjustment would help, and who could you consult if you were unsure? Keep this sheet private unless you actively choose to share it.

**After class:** Write a next learning step that does not require immediate physical practice. Identify a reputable reference and a qualified educator or professional when relevant.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
