# Accessibility & Adaptive BDSM

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `accessibility-and-adaptive-kink`
- Level: All experience levels
- Duration: 90 minutes; suggested maximum 20 learners
- Delivery: Inclusive workshop design and scenario planning
- Educator qualification: Educator with disability-access experience; ideally co-taught with disabled community educator
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. Recognize that accessibility is individualized, not a diagnosis-based list.
2. Ask about accommodations without demanding personal medical information.
3. Adapt classroom and play-space communication and positioning.
4. Identify equipment ratings, mobility barriers, fatigue and sensory needs.
5. Draft inclusive event instructions and an individual participation option.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. Hold all participant data confidentially and avoid identifiable anecdotes. Prepare an anonymized question route and ensure demonstrations remain within the announced scope.

**Bring:**
- Accessible seating examples and route map
- High contrast and large print handouts
- Sample nonverbal communication cards
- Consent and accommodation request template

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Welcome (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Disabled community members are experts in their own needs. Ask what works without making assumptions.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Offer accommodations and quiet options.

**Prompt / check:** Ask attendees to keep others' health details private.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Access as design (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Accessible entry, navigation, restrooms, lighting, seating, sound and communication affect whether people can participate.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Audit a fictional event description for missing information.

**Prompt / check:** Groups identify three barriers.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:40 · Consent and fatigue (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Fatigue, pain, medication, mobility and concentration can change across a day; a prior plan may need adaptation.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Demonstrate asking, 'What would make this activity workable?' without requesting diagnosis.

**Prompt / check:** Rewrite intrusive questions as access-oriented questions.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:40–00:55 · Adaptive positioning and equipment (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Not all chairs, benches or restraint points are rated or appropriate. Avoid improvisation without competence and structural verification.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Use photographs of equipment inspection labels and clearances; no participant restraint.

**Prompt / check:** Learners choose when to stop and consult qualified personnel.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:55–01:05 · Break (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Offer seated and quiet options.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Revisit communication needs.

**Prompt / check:** Collect anonymous questions.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:05–01:18 · Communication alternatives (13 minutes)

**Say:** “Provide audible, written and nonverbal options. Do not presume eye contact or speech is required.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Practice translating an instruction into large text and clear icons.

**Prompt / check:** Students create an alternative stop-signal protocol.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:18–01:26 · Inclusion without tokenization (8 minutes)

**Say:** “Invite suggestions and honor privacy. Do not make disabled attendees teach a class unless they chose that role.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Analyze a case in which a participant is singled out.

**Prompt / check:** Groups propose respectful accommodation changes.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:26–01:30 · Quiz and close (4 minutes)

**Say:** “Accessibility is an ongoing practice of consultation and revision.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Quiz and take-home audit checklist.

**Prompt / check:** Exit ticket: one barrier you can remove.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
