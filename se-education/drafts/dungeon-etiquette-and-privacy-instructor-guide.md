# Dungeon Etiquette, Privacy & Community Practice

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `dungeon-etiquette-and-privacy`
- Level: Beginner
- Duration: 90 minutes; suggested maximum 20 learners
- Delivery: Scenario-led community orientation, not venue admission authorization
- Educator qualification: SE organizer with verified venue-policy knowledge and safety monitor support
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. State the difference between event types, access eligibility and membership.
2. Apply ask-first practices for people, property and equipment.
3. Explain privacy rules and the limits of confidentiality.
4. Describe the appropriate way to contact a Dungeon Monitor.
5. Respond to bystander concerns without escalating a scene.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. Hold all participant data confidentially and avoid identifiable anecdotes. Prepare an anonymized question route and ensure demonstrations remain within the announced scope.

**Bring:**
- Current SE published venue guide
- Hypothetical incident cards
- Contact method for staff
- Floor map without publishing private address

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Opening and boundaries (10 minutes)

**Say:** “A venue is a shared space. Good etiquette protects everyone, including those who never participate in a scene.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Review event-specific eligibility and respectful language.

**Prompt / check:** Ask whether attendance itself implies permission to touch.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Space and movement (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Respect reserved areas, closed doors, furniture and accessibility routes.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Use conceptual floor diagram; explain how to approach staff.

**Prompt / check:** Students identify a safe way to ask whether a space is in use.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:40 · Consent and observation (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Watching a scene does not grant permission to intervene, photograph or comment. A monitor may intervene for safety.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Discuss negotiated spectator boundaries.

**Prompt / check:** Roleplay a polite observation request.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:40–00:55 · Privacy and digital conduct (15 minutes)

**Say:** “No one may photograph, record or identify others without their permission and applicable venue authorization.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Compare a consented public announcement with outing a member.

**Prompt / check:** Learners choose appropriate social-media handling.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:55–01:05 · Break (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Briefly revisit questions privately.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Collect anonymous cards.

**Prompt / check:** Check access requirements.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:05–01:18 · Safety monitors and incident paths (13 minutes)

**Say:** “If an emergency or concern arises, contact staff using the venue's reporting route. Do not physically intervene unless needed to prevent immediate harm.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Scenario: stopped scene, loud argument, inaccessible exit, suspected consent violation.

**Prompt / check:** Sort emergency response from after-event reporting.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:18–01:26 · Community repair and accountability (8 minutes)

**Say:** “Feedback can be candid while respecting process and privacy; an allegation is not a conclusion.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Discuss empathetic follow-up and nonretaliation.

**Prompt / check:** Write an actionable incident note using observed facts only.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:26–01:30 · Quiz and close (4 minutes)

**Say:** “An informed community member understands boundaries and knows how to get help.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Quiz.

**Prompt / check:** Exit ticket: name the correct reporting channel.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
