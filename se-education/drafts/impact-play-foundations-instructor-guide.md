# Impact Play Foundations

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `impact-play-foundations`
- Level: Beginner
- Duration: 120 minutes; suggested maximum 12 learners
- Delivery: Educational demonstration and supervised negotiation exercise; no unsupervised physical practice
- Educator qualification: Experienced impact educator; separate demo assistant and available first-aid responder
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. Differentiate common impact implements by surface, flexibility, and maintenance needs.
2. Identify why anatomy, disability, medication effects, and prior injury change the risk profile.
3. Negotiate intensity, areas excluded, duration, stop signals, and aftercare in clear terms.
4. Observe a qualified educator inspect an implement and demonstrate pacing on an inert target.
5. Describe indicators to stop and seek help rather than continuing a scene.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. Hold all participant data confidentially and avoid identifiable anecdotes. Prepare an anonymized question route and ensure demonstrations remain within the announced scope.

**Bring:**
- Clean display-only paddles, floggers, crops and inert target such as an appropriate practice cushion
- Anatomy illustration from a vetted licensed source
- Disinfectant compatible with each demonstration implement
- Printed negotiation and risk worksheets
- Accessible seating, water, and an emergency contact route

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Welcome and establish expectations (10 minutes)

**Say:** “This class teaches decision-making and observation before technique. Participation is optional, and no one needs to receive impact to learn.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Show exits, introductions by chosen name, sanitation protocol; explain that demonstration tools are handled only with permission.

**Prompt / check:** Ask: what distinguishes observation from permission to participate?

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Tools and material inspection (15 minutes)

**Say:** “A tool is not safe because it looks familiar. Condition, flexibility, length, and hygiene matter.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Show clean implements side by side; explain why splitting, sharp edges, debris, and damaged fittings mean retirement.

**Prompt / check:** Have learners identify two potential defects on photographs.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:45 · Body awareness and risk literacy (20 minutes)

**Say:** “There is no universally safe impact target, and there is no one-size-fits-all tolerance. Avoid high-risk regions, especially head, neck, spine, abdomen and kidneys.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Use a medically reviewed diagram to discuss vulnerability, bruising, tissue injury, and delayed symptoms without teaching risky targeting.

**Prompt / check:** Students annotate high-risk and excluded regions on a hypothetical scene plan.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:45–01:00 · Negotiation and intensity language (15 minutes)

**Say:** “A numerical scale is useful only when two people define it together. A request for more is never a waiver of agreed limits.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Roleplay a spoken negotiation covering first experience, medical considerations, excluded areas, check-ins and stop signals.

**Prompt / check:** Ask whether a tool change requires renewed agreement.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:00–01:10 · Break and questions (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Take a break; do not handle demonstration implements without the instructor.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Allow questions anonymously; inspect demonstration area.

**Prompt / check:** Check comfort and access needs.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:10–01:30 · Educator demonstration (20 minutes)

**Say:** “Watch how the educator measures, checks, communicates, pauses and stops. The demonstration is not an invitation to replicate this without training.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Demonstrate rhythm and variation on an inert target. Narrate distance checks, equipment inspection, and responsiveness; no participant is struck.

**Prompt / check:** Prompt observers to identify at least three decision points.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:30–01:47 · Risk scenario workshops (17 minutes)

**Say:** “If something unexpected happens, stopping is the right first decision; do not continue to 'test' symptoms.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Groups analyze scenarios: numbness, emotional distress, damaged tool, different requested implement.

**Prompt / check:** Have learners write immediate stop, care and reporting choices.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:47–01:56 · Aftercare and injuries (9 minutes)

**Say:** “Care is negotiated, not imposed. Persistent or concerning symptoms require appropriate medical evaluation.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Compare quiet time, water, comfort, privacy and later check-in. Discuss red flags and event incident response.

**Prompt / check:** Students draft a two-sentence aftercare agreement.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:56–02:00 · Assessment and close (4 minutes)

**Say:** “Your ability to stop a scene matters more than memorizing a list of implements.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Collect knowledge check and provide further learning resources.

**Prompt / check:** Exit ticket: name one boundary and one stop condition.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

4. **Outcome 4:** Observe a qualified educator inspect an implement and demonstrate pacing on an inert target. Write a hypothetical example and one question you would ask before acting.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
