# Rope & Restraint Fundamentals

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `rope-restraint-fundamentals`
- Level: Beginner
- Duration: 120 minutes; suggested maximum 12 learners
- Delivery: Tabletop rope handling, verbal negotiation, non-suspension introduction only
- Educator qualification: Experienced rope educator with demonstrated emergency release proficiency
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. Compare common rope and cuff materials and maintenance needs.
2. Explain why compression, restricted circulation, nerve pressure, falls, and escape access matter.
3. Describe the anatomy-based reason not to leave someone restrained unattended.
4. Demonstrate an emergency release from a practice object rather than a person.
5. Draft a restraint negotiation with positions excluded, signs of distress and an exit plan.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. Hold all participant data confidentially and avoid identifiable anecdotes. Prepare an anonymized question route and ensure demonstrations remain within the announced scope.

**Bring:**
- Clean teaching rope samples and synthetic straps
- Safety shears available for discussion
- Inert foam practice cylinder and cuffs for inspection
- Illustrations of nerve and circulatory warning signs
- Accessible workspace with chairs and exit clearance

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Opening and consent boundaries (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Restraint is not simply a knot. The questions are whether this person, in this position, can remain safe, communicate and be released.”

**Do / demonstrate:** State no suspension, no unattended restraint and no surprise tying. Invite opt-outs.

**Prompt / check:** Check: what should a student do if they do not want physical participation?

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Tools, rope and hardware (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Materials differ in grip, friction, stretch, cleaning and deterioration.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Compare rope materials, buckles, connectors and shears without applying to bodies.

**Prompt / check:** Learners list wear defects and contaminated materials.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:45 · Nerve, blood-flow and positioning risks (20 minutes)

**Say:** “Pins and needles, loss of sensation, unusual pain, color changes or weakness are reasons to stop, release and evaluate.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Use non-identifying anatomy visuals. Discuss pressure over vulnerable nerves, falls and impaired mobility.

**Prompt / check:** Learners identify why 'tight but comfortable at first' is not enough.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:45–01:00 · Negotiation and communication (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Agree on restraint objective, permitted positions, supervision, signals, release triggers and emergency access.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Facilitator performs verbal negotiation example; no touching.

**Prompt / check:** Students prepare a fictional restraint checklist.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:00–01:10 · Break (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Make the equipment safe and return for the tabletop portion.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Clear ropes and remind learners no solo body restraints.

**Prompt / check:** Take anonymous questions.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:10–01:30 · Non-body emergency release lab (20 minutes)

**Say:** “A rehearsed exit is part of the plan, not an afterthought.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Instructors demonstrate quick release with inert object and safe shears handling away from skin.

**Prompt / check:** Students describe the sequence out loud; tactile practice only on inert objects.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:30–01:45 · Scenario decisions (15 minutes)

**Say:** “If communication fails or symptoms emerge, stop and release promptly.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Analyze hypothetical dizziness, tingling, panic, a fall risk and blocked exit.

**Prompt / check:** Each group chooses the first action and who calls for assistance.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:45–01:55 · Comfort and aftercare (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Pressure, awkward posture and anxiety may be felt later; follow up and note symptoms.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Review comfort, hydration, mobility assistance, and when professional assessment is appropriate.

**Prompt / check:** Create a personalized release and care plan.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:55–02:00 · Quiz and referral (5 minutes)

**Say:** “This class does not qualify someone for suspension, advanced ties or medical procedures.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Assessment and educator recommendations for further supervised practice.

**Prompt / check:** Collect one unresolved question anonymously.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
