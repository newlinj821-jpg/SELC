# Aftercare, Drop & Follow-Up

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `aftercare-and-drop`
- Level: Beginner
- Duration: 90 minutes; suggested maximum 20 learners
- Delivery: Discussion, personal planning and scenario exercises
- Educator qualification: Kink educator familiar with aftercare; healthcare guest for medical questions if available
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. Differentiate immediate aftercare and later follow-up.
2. Describe that emotional shifts may affect tops, bottoms and observers.
3. Identify supportive care without assuming touch or intimacy.
4. Recognize urgent symptoms that warrant professional medical care.
5. Draft an individualized support and follow-up plan.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. Hold all participant data confidentially and avoid identifiable anecdotes. Prepare an anonymized question route and ensure demonstrations remain within the announced scope.

**Bring:**
- Blank care cards
- Water and quiet area
- Accessible electronic/printed action worksheet
- Professional referral list

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Opening (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Aftercare is negotiated support, not a ritual everyone has to perform. It can involve quiet, physical comfort, distance or conversation.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Describe opt-outs, privacy and non-clinical education.

**Prompt / check:** Ask whether aftercare always requires touch.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Physiology and emotion (15 minutes)

**Say:** “High arousal, fatigue, pain, stress and relationship context can shape how people feel afterward. Reactions vary.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Explain possible emotional and physical changes without diagnosing or promising a universal timeline.

**Prompt / check:** Invite fictional example discussion.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:40 · Before-the-scene planning (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Ask each person what support they prefer and what they do not want.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Model an aftercare negotiation with differing preferences.

**Prompt / check:** Students create 'helpful / not helpful / ask first' cards.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:40–00:55 · Immediate response (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Comfort needs are different from medical emergencies. Assess alertness, breathing and injury; seek emergency services for severe symptoms.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Walk through non-contact scenarios: dizziness, emotional distress, someone wanting solitude.

**Prompt / check:** Have learners identify escalation decisions.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:55–01:05 · Break (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Use quiet room or ask anonymous questions.”

**Do / demonstrate:** No disclosure required.

**Prompt / check:** Check access needs.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:05–01:17 · Later check-ins (12 minutes)

**Say:** “A next-day message can be useful if agreed. No one owes another person affection, continued intimacy or public reassurance.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Draft a two-sentence follow-up message and consent to receive it.

**Prompt / check:** Discuss boundaries around messaging.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:17–01:26 · Drop and professional support (9 minutes)

**Say:** “Some experience low mood or unease after a scene. Persistent or severe distress merits appropriate mental-health support.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Discuss stigma-free referral and non-therapeutic peer support.

**Prompt / check:** Create a 'what I will do if worried' plan.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:26–01:30 · Quiz and close (4 minutes)

**Say:** “A good care plan is specific, revisable and freely agreed.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Collect quick assessment.

**Prompt / check:** Exit ticket: one preferred check-in option.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
