# Power Exchange & D/s Relationships

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `power-exchange-and-ds`
- Level: All experience levels
- Duration: 90 minutes; suggested maximum 20 learners
- Delivery: Interactive discussion, written agreements and boundaries exercises
- Educator qualification: Experienced D/s educator or facilitator comfortable with diverse nonhierarchical relationships
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. Distinguish negotiated authority from unilateral control.
2. Describe consent, autonomy and accountability in D/s.
3. Compare time-limited scene roles with recurring dynamics.
4. Write a hypothetical agreement covering boundaries, review dates and exit options.
5. Recognize coercive warning signs and how to seek support.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. - Anonymous question method

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Welcome and community diversity (10 minutes)

**Say:** “There is no single authentic Dominant or submissive model. Relationships and scenes are shaped by explicit agreements.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Invite language preferences without asking for personal histories.

**Prompt / check:** Ask: can two consenting people practice a different structure than your own?

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Roles, authority and agency (15 minutes)

**Say:** “D/s can involve structured decision-making, but power exchange does not abolish an individual's rights to privacy, medical care or refusal.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Compare examples of service, protocol, daily structure and scene-only roles.

**Prompt / check:** Have learners classify a choice as negotiated or imposed.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:40 · Scope and agreements (15 minutes)

**Say:** “An agreement needs subject matter, boundaries, review points and an exit option. Title or collar alone is not an unlimited authorization.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Discuss hypothetical terms including contact, chores, discipline, money and communication.

**Prompt / check:** Students rewrite a vague agreement into clear limited clauses.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:40–00:55 · Consent and changing circumstances (15 minutes)

**Say:** “People change. Work, fatigue, illness and social obligations can change what is feasible or desirable.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Roleplay a pause request and compassionate response.

**Prompt / check:** Ask how to renegotiate when expectations no longer fit.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:55–01:05 · Break (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Take a break; confidential questions welcome.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Collect scenario prompts.

**Prompt / check:** Invite quiet reflection.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:05–01:18 · Coercion and accountability (13 minutes)

**Say:** “Using status to isolate, threaten or deny outside help is not evidence of healthy D/s.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Read case studies: punished for setting a medical limit, coerced disclosure, isolation from friends.

**Prompt / check:** Have attendees identify warning signs and ways to find help.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:18–01:26 · Durability and check-ins (8 minutes)

**Say:** “Well-designed dynamics can include explicit review meetings, shared expectations and repair after misunderstanding.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Build an agreement review checklist.

**Prompt / check:** Students choose a monthly review prompt.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:26–01:30 · Assessment and conclusion (4 minutes)

**Say:** “Responsible power exchange includes responsibility for consent, communication and repair.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Quiz and closing resources.

**Prompt / check:** Exit ticket: name an exit clause.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
