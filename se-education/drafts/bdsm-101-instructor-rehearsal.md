# SE Education / BDSM 101 — Instructor rehearsal draft v0.1
**Status: development only. Not published to students.** Teaching guidance for a 90-minute, discussion-based class. Faculty editor should review all content before use.

## Class definition
Audience: Adults new to kink or wishing to review fundamentals. No prior experience. No nudity, contact or participation in scenes required. Instructor: approved educator with working knowledge of community consent practices, local venue policy, and trauma-aware facilitation. A co-facilitator is advisable for a larger class.

**Learning outcomes.** At the end, participants can: (1) distinguish practices, roles and relationship structures; (2) articulate that consent is specific, informed, ongoing and revocable; (3) walk through a four-part scene negotiation; (4) name two health or environmental hazards and corresponding controls; (5) describe how to stop or pause activity; and (6) identify a personalized aftercare need and a follow-up action.

### Preparation 24–48 hours before
- Verify room maximum occupancy, seating, accessible entrance, accessible restroom, lighting and sound, water, and clearly marked exits. Ensure paper/accessible digital worksheets, pens, and visible high-contrast slides.
- Have blank scenario cards, consent practice sheets, a private feedback form, facilitator timer, and a printed referral/resource sheet. Never request names/identifiers on exercise sheets.
- Remove play equipment from the learning floor unless purposefully introduced for a static show-and-tell. No practice hitting, tying, electrical demonstrations, choking or breath restriction.
- Set a neutral attendee standard: no touching without asking; no participant is obligated to disclose relationship status, past trauma or sexual history; people may step outside without explaining.
- Review venue escalation protocol; know who handles personal conflict, medical emergencies and accommodations. Do not present yourself as a clinician or attorney.
- Screen any material against SE's 18+/21+ location-specific attendance rules and posted event standards.

## Minute-by-minute facilitator script

### 00:00–00:08 — Arrival and learning agreement
**Say:** “Welcome to BDSM 101, presented through SE Education. Today is an introduction, not a test of how experienced or adventurous anyone is. We will talk about community practices, informed choices, and how people communicate. No one is required to demonstrate anything, explain a limit, reveal a kink, or touch someone else. If you need a break, step out and return whenever you want.”

**Do:** Point out exits, restrooms, water and accessibility resources. Ask participants to use first names, scene names, or no name at all. State the class is discussion only and does not authorize play at the event. Offer anonymous question cards. Read the learning agreement visibly.

**Check:** “What would make the room easier to learn in? You can tell us privately, too.” Avoid requiring personal disclosure.

### 00:08–00:20 — Vocabulary without assuming identities
**Say:** “BDSM is an umbrella, not one prescribed experience. People can enjoy bondage, discipline, dominance, submission, sadism, masochism, or other activities without treating those interests as their entire identity. A top does an action; a bottom receives it. Dominant and submissive often describe negotiated authority. Those categories overlap, but they are not synonyms.”

**Do:** Write on board: activities; roles in an activity; relationship structures. Show an example: “One person can top an impact scene without being another person's Dominant. An ongoing D/s relationship may involve no impact at all.” Explain that switch is a self-description, not a requirement to experience both roles.

**Ask:** “Can a submissive be the person who sets a hard limit?” Expected: yes; everyone retains agency and an unconditional ability to stop.

### 00:20–00:35 — Consent mechanics
**Say:** “Consent is a decision made with enough information and genuine freedom to decline. Agreeing last week is not agreeing now. Agreeing to one kind of touch is not blanket permission for another. Silence, freezing, or an inability to respond cannot be treated as permission.”

**Do:** Contrast four examples on separate cards: (A) clear agreement to a described activity; (B) an agreement without agreeing on intensity; (C) a changed mind partway through; (D) a partner too impaired to make an informed decision. Invite discussion without asking people to describe their own incidents.

**Ask:** “In example C, what happens next?” Expected: immediately stop the activity; check what the person needs; do not negotiate against their withdrawal.

**Clarify:** A prior D/s agreement, roleplay or claimed ‘consent by default’ does not supersede active permission and safewords.

### 00:35–00:50 — Negotiation demonstration (spoken only)
**Say:** “Before a scene, people should clarify what is proposed, what is not proposed, what would end it, and what care might be useful afterward. You cannot responsibly negotiate with vague assumptions.”

**Do:** Co-facilitator reads:
- Facilitator A: “I'd like to explore light, nonsexual sensation play, for up to five minutes. Are you interested?”
- Facilitator B: “Only on my forearm; no surprises. I want to stop immediately if I say red.”
- A: “Agreed. If you say yellow, we pause and check in. Is there anything else I should know?”
- B: “Please ask before changing a sensation or adding a new tool.”
- A: “Yes. Afterward, I'd prefer a few minutes alone, then water.”
**Debrief:** Identify activity, body boundaries, time limit, signal, check-ins, and aftercare. Mention that real hazards must be evaluated separately for any physical practice.

**Exercise:** Pairs or solo attendees fill a hypothetical plan. No personal touch or explicit disclosures. Offer to observe rather than pair up.

### 00:50–01:00 — Break
**Say:** “Take ten minutes. You can ask questions privately. We are not collecting personal scene plans.”

### 01:00–01:15 — Risk awareness and stop conditions
**Say:** “No tool and no activity is free of risk. Equipment, body position, prior injury, medication effects, emotional state, attention, sanitation, environment, and the qualifications of the people involved all change the risk.”

**Do:** Display a simple chart: activity; possible harm; safeguards; stop condition. For example: restraint → nerve pressure or circulation concerns → prompt release, frequent checks and avoiding high-risk arrangements → immediately stop for numbness, tingling, unusual pain, color changes, dizziness, or distress. Do not teach suspension in a beginner course. Impact activity → bruising or tissue injury → trained positioning and clear intensity agreement → stop for distress, concerning pain, or unexpected symptoms. Avoid presenting risky body areas as ‘safe to hit’.

**Emphasize:** Breath restriction and neck compression carry risks of serious injury and death; do not demonstrate or normalize them as beginner activities. Serious or persistent symptoms require medical assessment.

### 01:15–01:23 — Aftercare and follow-up
**Say:** “Aftercare is not one prescribed ritual. Some people want water and quiet; some want conversation; others don't want touch. It is best negotiated rather than assumed. Check-ins later may be helpful as emotions or physical soreness change.”

**Do:** Give choices: physical comfort, hydration, space, reassurance, transport plan, checking injuries, next-day message. Ask attendees to compose a fictitious aftercare plan that respects autonomy. Discuss that neither participant owes affection as a ‘reward’ or continuation of a scene.

### 01:23–01:28 — Knowledge check
Hand out the six-question quiz below (or use secure Portal quiz if deployed). Tell attendees this is formative, not an approval or certification to practice a high-risk skill.

### 01:28–01:30 — Close
**Say:** “The useful thing to take away is not a list of techniques; it's a habit: ask, listen, negotiate clearly, notice change, stop when needed, and follow up. Learning does not end with this session. If you want to explore a practice, choose qualified instruction appropriate to the risk.”

**Do:** Share next class subjects, confidential ways to report a concern, resource links, anonymous feedback link and contact for accommodation requests.

## Assessment bank — private instructor key
1. **Can someone revoke permission after agreeing to a scene?** Correct: Yes, any time. Explanation: prior agreement does not make consent irrevocable.
2. **Does calling someone your submissive authorize activities not discussed?** Correct: No. Explanation: a title or relationship status is not authorization.
3. **Choose the clearest negotiation.** Options: “Anything goes”; “We'll see”; “Five minutes of named activity, agreed boundaries, stop signals and aftercare”; “No need to talk.” Correct: third.
4. **If a participant becomes unresponsive or freezes, what should happen?** Correct: stop, ensure immediate safety, assess need for emergency services. Explanation: inability to communicate is not permission.
5. **A participant reports tingling or numbness during restraint.** Correct: stop and release promptly, assess symptoms and obtain appropriate medical attention. Explanation: symptoms can indicate nerve or circulation injury.
6. **Is aftercare always cuddling?** Correct: No. Explanation: individual preferences vary; ask before initiating touch.
Suggested pass: 5/6, with review of any missed safety question. Quiz success is not technical certification.

## Facilitator contingencies
**Participant asks for a demonstration of choking:** “We do not demonstrate breath restriction in this introductory class. Neck pressure and reduced oxygen have severe risks; we can share reputable safety information and discuss safer alternatives.”
**Participant jokes that safewords ‘ruin the mood’:** “A stop signal is part of the agreement. Ignoring it is a consent violation, regardless of whether the activity was roleplayed.”
**Participant discloses harm in front of the room:** “Thank you for telling us. You do not have to share more publicly. Let's connect privately with the appropriate reporting resources after this section.” Do not investigate publicly.
**Participant says ‘I do not know my limits’:** “That is a valid starting point. It means you should choose low-stakes discussion and education rather than assuming agreement to an unknown practice.”
**Class runs 10 minutes late:** shorten group reports and case examples, not the consent/stop-signal section or conclusion.
