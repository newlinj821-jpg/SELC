# Dungeon Monitor Foundations

**SE Education · Complete instructor rehearsal draft v1 · NOT APPROVED / NOT PUBLISHED**

- Course ID: `dungeon-monitor-foundations`
- Level: Intermediate
- Duration: 120 minutes; suggested maximum 16 learners
- Delivery: Scenario tabletop training with live role-based communication exercises
- Educator qualification: Senior qualified DM or safety leader with first-aid response protocols
- Student access: only approved, confirmed enrollment in linked Workshop event; teacher plan never exposed to learners

## Learner outcomes
1. Describe monitor authority, role boundaries, escalation and documentation.
2. Distinguish nonurgent etiquette concerns from imminent safety threats.
3. Practice immediate, proportionate interventions and de-escalation.
4. Demonstrate accurate objective incident notes.
5. Explain confidentiality, emergency communication and post-event handoff.

## Instructor preparation
Use SE's current published rules, verify the event room and staff coverage, identify emergency contacts and quiet space, confirm an accessible way to participate without disclosure or touch, and obtain educator/SELC approval. Hold all participant data confidentially and avoid identifiable anecdotes. Prepare an anonymized question route and ensure demonstrations remain within the announced scope.

**Bring:**
- Current SE policies and incident forms
- Role badges for tabletop exercises
- Radio/communication examples
- Emergency plan and first-aid referral information

## Verbatim facilitator run-of-show

### 00:00–00:10 · Welcome and authority (10 minutes)

**Say:** “A monitor protects consent and safety, not personal preferences. Interventions must follow venue policy and be proportionate.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Define duty areas, handoff and emergency channels.

**Prompt / check:** Ask who can override a scene in immediate danger under policy.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:10–00:25 · Pre-event risk audit (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Check exits, fire hazards, hygiene supplies, equipment wear, occupancy, and staffing.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Walk through a sample room diagram.

**Prompt / check:** Learners identify urgent versus routine defects.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:25–00:40 · Observing without policing (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Look for risk signals without assuming every unconventional practice is a violation. Respect negotiated boundaries while checking safety.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Analyze case cards with ambiguous information.

**Prompt / check:** Practice neutral observation language.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:40–00:55 · Interruption and de-escalation (15 minutes)

**Say:** “Approach calmly, identify yourself, describe the specific concern, and request a pause when necessary. Call assistance for dangerous situations.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Two instructors roleplay a blocked exit and a disagreement; no physical contact.

**Prompt / check:** Peers practice non-accusatory scripts.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 00:55–01:05 · Break (10 minutes)

**Say:** “Reset scenarios.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Private questions.

**Prompt / check:** Check accessibility.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:05–01:25 · Medical and emergency response (20 minutes)

**Say:** “Know scope: call emergency services for serious symptoms; first aid only within training. Preserve scene safety and clear access.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Tabletop events: fall, loss of responsiveness, suspected injury, fire alarm.

**Prompt / check:** Teams produce first 60-second action checklist.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:25–01:43 · Incident reports (18 minutes)

**Say:** “Record observed actions, times, who was informed and immediate decisions. Keep conclusions and rumors separate.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Write anonymized mock reports.

**Prompt / check:** Peer review for specificity and privacy.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:43–01:55 · Handoff and follow-up (12 minutes)

**Say:** “Staff must know what remains unresolved and what information requires restricted access.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Practice a shift handoff with a supervisor.

**Prompt / check:** Students prepare a concise status handoff.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

### 01:55–02:00 · Quiz and close (5 minutes)

**Say:** “This class is a training foundation, not authorization for advanced medical or restraint intervention.”

**Do / demonstrate:** Quiz; discuss practical observation requirements and any separate certification.

**Prompt / check:** One unanswered risk question.

**If someone declines:** Thank them, offer silent observation or private writing, and continue without calling attention to the choice. **If a safety issue arises:** Stop the exercise, secure the situation, summon appropriate venue assistance, and avoid improvising medical interventions beyond training.

## Instructor scenarios and contingency responses

- **“Can we skip negotiation if we know each other?”** Say: “Familiarity may help communication, but does not replace current permission. We will practice clear questions regardless.”
- **“Can I just watch?”** Say: “Yes. Watching is a valid way to participate, and you can decline discussion, demonstration or practice.”
- **A participant asks about a technique beyond this class.** Say: “That is outside this class's competency and risk scope. I can point you toward an appropriately qualified educator rather than improvising.”
- **A participant begins sharing details about someone else.** Say: “Please keep identifying details out of class discussion. Use a hypothetical version, or raise a confidential concern through the proper channel.”
- **A participant appears distressed.** Pause, offer choice and privacy, avoid requiring disclosure, and connect with appropriate staff or clinical/emergency support if needed.

## Classroom evaluation rubric

An effective participant can explain a relevant consent decision, identify a limit and a stop condition, describe an option that respects accessibility, and communicate when outside expertise is required. Educators should record what was confusing, not personal scene disclosures.

**Teacher self-review after class:** Were the learning outcomes met? Did anyone struggle with access or vocabulary? What activities ran long? Which safety clarifications were necessary? What sources require refreshing? Record revisions under a new version before repeating the class.
